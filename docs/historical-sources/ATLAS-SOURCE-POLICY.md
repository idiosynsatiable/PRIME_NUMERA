# Symbolic Atlas Source Policy

The Symbolic Atlas is not a bucket called “ancient numerology.”

Every module must identify what kind of system or material it actually contains, including:

- runic writing
- calendar or calendrical mathematics
- symbolic writing
- historical divination
- mythology
- folklore
- modern reconstruction

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in roughly this order:

1. primary historical sources or responsible critical editions
2. cultural heritage authorities, museums, and community institutions
3. academic secondary literature
4. documented modern practitioners for explicitly modern/living material
5. editorial content only for clearly labeled modern/entertainment layers

Every factual or interpretive claim carries source IDs and a claim-status classification:

- attested
- scholarly reconstruction
- disputed
- modern
- living tradition

## Indigenous and living-tradition rule

Do not use a generic “Native American” or “Indigenous numerology” category.

Modules involving Indigenous peoples must be nation/community/history specific and should prioritize sources that include community voices. Sacred, restricted, ceremonial, or community-sensitive practices are not automatically available for entertainment adaptation.

## Current foundation modules

### Scandinavian Runic Writing

Initial authoritative anchors:

- Swedish National Heritage Board, Runor research platform: https://www.raa.se/in-english/digital-services/about-runor/
- Swedish National Heritage Board, reading runes and inscriptions: https://www.raa.se/kulturarv/runor-och-runstenar/att-lasa-runor-och-runinskrifter/
- Swedish National Heritage Board, rune school: https://www.raa.se/kulturarv/runor-och-runstenar/runskolan/allmant-om-runor/

The Atlas treats runes first as historical writing signs with phonetic/linguistic and inscriptional context. Modern divinatory rune meanings, where included later, must be a separately labeled modern/entertainment layer.

### Maya Calendrical Systems

Initial authoritative/community-inclusive anchors:

- Smithsonian NMAI Living Maya Time calendar: https://maya.nmai.si.edu/calendar
- Calendar system: https://maya.nmai.si.edu/calendar/calendar-system
- Calendar converter / Long Count arithmetic: https://maya.nmai.si.edu/calendar/maya-calendar-converter
- Maya peoples: https://maya.nmai.si.edu/maya

The Atlas treats the Haab, Tzolk'in, Calendar Round, and Long Count as calendrical systems. It does not relabel them “Maya numerology.” The source set includes contemporary Maya voices and explicitly treats Maya culture as living.
